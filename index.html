Foveated Compression: Selective High-Resolution Preservation for Token-Efficient VLMs

Donghyun Han1*†, Jangho Park2*, Yuseok Bae1
1ETRI, South Korea 2Kyung Hee University, South Korea
NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: VLM4RWD

Paper arXiv Code Poster

Under a fixed visual-token budget, where should a VLM keep native resolution? Foveated Compression encodes an image once, keeps one selected region at native fidelity, and compresses the rest, so the model keeps both global coverage and local detail at 20.99% of the visual tokens.

Abstract

Visual tokens are a major source of inference cost in vision-language models, yet simple image downsampling remains a surprisingly strong compression baseline. This raises a complementary question: under a fixed token budget, where should visual fidelity be preserved? We introduce Foveated Compression, which encodes a full-resolution image once and represents it with a mixture of native- and compressed-resolution visual tokens. A behaviorally self-distilled Foveated Merger compresses local visual tokens while preserving compatibility with their native counterparts, and a lightweight Foveated Selector chooses one of nine spatial cells to retain at native resolution using exhaustive budget-matched intervention supervision. Token budgets count visual tokens entering the language model, which govern prefill cost and KV-cache memory; the vision encoder still runs once at full resolution. At 11.11% visual tokens, uniform Foveated Compression shows no significant paired difference from iso-token downsampling. At 20.99%, the learned selector significantly outperforms random and fixed allocation, but remains below strong whole-image resizing, showing that localized fidelity is not universally preferable. Within the same 3×3, K=1 action space, a budget-matched region-choice oracle reaches 82.73 macro accuracy versus 69.61 for the learned selector, revealing substantial unrealized headroom. Matched probing further shows that signals predicting when compression breaks the answer are substantially more accessible after language-model computation than to the lightweight prefill-free selector. These results expose complementary bottlenecks in region selection and compressed-region fidelity.

Where Does Resolution Matter?

Visual tokens drive prefill cost and KV-cache memory, and plain image downsampling already matches many elaborate token-compression methods. Yet native detail is rarely needed everywhere in an image. This turns the usual question of how many tokens to keep into a different one:

Method

The image is encoded once by the frozen vision backbone, the Foveated Merger produces a globally compressed representation, and the Foveated Selector restores one selected spatial cell with native visual tokens through in-place replacement.

Foveated Merger

Compresses each 3×3 block of native visual tokens into one token (11.11% of native), before Qwen's frozen visual merger. It starts exactly from mean pooling and is trained by behavioral self-distillation: a KL loss against the frozen model's own full-resolution answers, with no ground-truth labels. 32.77M parameters.

Foveated Selector

A lightweight, prefill-free scorer that reads native tokens, compressed tokens, and question embeddings, then ranks nine coarse cells. It is supervised by running all nine budget-matched K=1 interventions and keeping tied winners as sets (tie-aware loss). 10.8M parameters, no language-model pass.

Mixed-Resolution Sequence

The selected cell's compressed tokens are replaced in place by their native tokens. Both scales share the native M-RoPE grid, and the sequence costs exactly 17/81 = 20.99% of native tokens whichever cell is chosen, with no re-encoding.

  • The merger matches resizing at equal cost. At 11.11% tokens, uniform Foveated Compression and iso-token downsampling show no significant paired difference (95% CI [−0.586, 0.521]), while the merger additionally keeps native-token compatibility.
  • Spatial preferences are learnable. At 20.99%, the selector beats random allocation by +1.62 and training-only fixed allocation by +1.13 macro points; both 95% CIs exclude zero.
  • Localized fidelity is not universally better. Strong whole-image resizing still leads on average. The selector wins on ChartQA (57.84 vs. 53.48), while resizing wins clearly on OCRBench and MME.
  • Large headroom remains. Within the same 3×3, K=1 action space, the budget-matched oracle is 13.12 points above the learned selector.

Why Is Selection Hard?

We probe whether a signal can predict when compression breaks a correct answer, on 3,416 matched examples (chance = 0.50 AUC). Failure signals are weak before prefill and strong after it: the information the selector would need is substantially more accessible after language-model computation, which a single-pass, prefill-free selector cannot use.

Takeaways

  1. One encoding serves two resolutions. Compressed and native tokens come from the same full-resolution pass, so any region can be restored without re-encoding the image.
  2. Allocation is learnable, but not yet dominant. Learned allocation beats random and fixed regions, yet strong whole-image resizing still leads on average.
  3. The 13-point oracle gap is an open problem. Closing it likely needs selection signals that draw on language-model computation, together with stronger compressed representations.

Limitations. Token ratios count visual tokens entering the language model, not end-to-end compute: the vision encoder still runs once at full resolution, while pixel-space downsampling also shrinks encoder cost. Results cover one frozen backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and the 3×3, K=1 action space only.
